## **What is Trace?**

Soundverse Trace measures **how similar two songs are, and in what way**. Instead of a single yes/no match, it compares songs on separate layers (the full mix, the individual instruments, the song's structure, its melody, its lyrics and its style) and returns a score for each layer, along with the evidence behind it, such as which moments of the two songs line up.

Typical uses:

*   **Copyright and catalog screening**: check a new track against your catalog before release.
*   **Cover, remix and sample detection**: find where a melody, a vocal or an instrument part was reused.
*   **Catalog matching**: compare one catalog against another, every song against every song.

<Note>
  Audio fingerprinting answers "is this the same recording?". Trace answers "how similar are these two songs, layer by layer?", so it can surface reuse that an exact-match fingerprint would miss.
</Note>

## **How It Works**

Trace works in two stages. **Ingestion** does the heavy audio analysis once per song and stores the result. **Search** then compares the stored analysis, so you can run as many searches as you need without reprocessing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your audio">
    Upload each song with `POST /v1/files` and keep the returned `file_id`. See [Upload Audio](/enterprise-api/trace/upload).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a dataset">
    A dataset is a named collection of songs that you search against. See [Datasets](/enterprise-api/trace/datasets).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ingest songs into the dataset">
    Choose which layers to prepare. Trace analyses each song once. See [Ingest Audio](/enterprise-api/trace/ingest).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run a search">
    Pick a [Custom Search](/enterprise-api/trace/search), [Comprehensive Search](/enterprise-api/trace/comprehensive-search) or a [Use-Case Search](/enterprise-api/trace/use-case-search). Each returns a `job_id` immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get the results">
    Poll the job until it completes, then read the scores. See [Job Status & Results](/enterprise-api/trace/search-status).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A **1:1** search (one song against one song) works directly on file IDs. You only need a dataset and ingestion for **1:N** and **M:N** searches.
</Note>

## **Search Shapes**

Every search route accepts the same three shapes, set with `search_type`:

| `search_type` | Compares | Required fields |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `1:1` | One song against one song | `query_file_id`, `candidate_file_id` |
| `1:n` | One song against every song in a dataset | `query_file_id`, `candidate_dataset_name` |
| `m:n` | Every song in one dataset against every song in another | `query_dataset_name`, `candidate_dataset_name` |

## **Layers (Depths)**

A **depth** is one layer of comparison. You choose depths in a [Custom Search](/enterprise-api/trace/search); [Comprehensive](/enterprise-api/trace/comprehensive-search) and [Use-Case](/enterprise-api/trace/use-case-search) searches choose them for you. A dataset can only be searched at the depths it was ingested with.

| Depth | What it compares | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `track` | The full mix | Overall sound, production and genre |
| `light_stem` | The vocals and the accompaniment, separately | Reuse of the same vocal recording |
| `stem` | Vocals, bass, drums and other instruments, separately | A lifted bassline, drum loop or vocal |
| `section` | Matching sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro) | Structure and harmony, part by part |
| `stem_section` | Sections within each instrument (search only) | Detailed structural comparison |
| `motif` | Short melodic phrases | Melody reuse. In `1:n` and `m:n` searches, also when the melody is moved to another key |
| `lyrics` | The transcribed lyrics | Covers and reused lyrics |
| `translation` | The lyrics after translating both songs to English | Covers in another language |
| `insights` | Genre, instrument and style tags | Style proximity |

<Note>
  A high score on the vocal stem means the **same vocal recording** was reused. It does not identify the same singer singing something new.
</Note>

| Search | Endpoint | Use it when |

## **Reading Scores**

Scores range from `0` (no similarity) to `1` (identical); `percentage` is the same value from 0 to 100. Trace cuts each song into 16 windows of 8 seconds, compares every window of one song with every window of the other, and reports:

| Field | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `hungarian_mean` | **The headline score.** The best one-to-one pairing of windows, averaged |
| `max_then_mean` | Each window's closest match, averaged (several windows may pick the same partner) |
| `max` | The single most similar pair of windows |

Melody (`motif`) results report the **number of matching phrases** rather than a percentage. Scores are raw similarity values, not legal or copyright determinations.

## **What to Expect**

*   **Ingestion and search are asynchronous.** Every `POST` returns a `job_id` at once; poll it for the result.
*   **Ingestion is incremental.** Layers a song already has are skipped. Adding a new depth later only processes that depth.
*   **Billing is per job and based on usage.** Ingestion and search are charged when the job completes; failed jobs are not charged. Uploads, dataset management and status checks are free. See [Errors & Billing](/enterprise-api/trace/errors-and-billing).
*   **Large results are delivered separately.** Results over about 200 KB (common for M:N) are fetched through [Full Results](/enterprise-api/trace/job-results).

## **Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)**

### Do I need a dataset to compare two songs?
No. A `1:1` search compares two uploaded files directly. Datasets are needed for `1:n` and `m:n` searches.

### How do I map a result back to my own file?
Each match is identified by a Trace audio ID (for example `d79efad8…c0466.mp3`). Call [`GET /trace/v3/dataset/{dataset_id}/tracks`](/enterprise-api/trace/datasets): every track lists its `audio_id` next to `source_ref`, which is the `file_id` you uploaded.

### What happened to `type1` and `type2`?
They were earlier Trace models and have been retired. Trace now runs a single model, which appears as `type3` in dataset responses. You do not choose a model in requests.

### Why are ingestion and search asynchronous?
Audio analysis and large comparisons can take from seconds to several minutes. Returning a `job_id` immediately avoids request timeouts and lets you poll at your own pace.

### What does a search cost?
Searches and ingestion are billed by usage when the job completes. The amount depends on the songs' length, the layers involved and the number of comparisons. Failed jobs are not charged. Your balance is shown at [platform.soundverse.ai](https://platform.soundverse.ai).
