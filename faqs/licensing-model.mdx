<Accordion title="What is Royalty Free?">
Royalty-free music allows you to incorporate it into your content without recurring royalty payments to the artists or rights holders each time it's played. However, this doesn't necessarily cover public performance rights, so there may still be royalty fees when you release it on digital platforms.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I buy a license for one track?">
Open the track's page and click **License**. You can choose:
- **Standard**: included with every track, for personal and non-commercial use.
- **Commercial**: monetize the track on streaming, social and ads, up to 1,000,000 streams or impressions. Not exclusive.
- **Full ownership**: exclusive transfer of full commercial rights. The track is removed from the Soundverse catalog and won't be licensed to anyone else.

The License window shows the price in your currency. After you buy, you get a license certificate with an ID anyone can verify.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What is the Sample Usage license?">
- Samples User License- Under this agreement, the users can distribute samples generated by Soundverse as part of a creation. Users on the Pro and Max plans have this license by default.

- This will cover usage of License in your own creations as long as Sample doesn't become the final product.
- You can use this sample in your own creations. You're allowed to distribute it to Spotify/ Apple Music, as long as this sample usage doesn't make 100% of your final track.
- By distribution here, we mean you can distribute sample that's part of your creation. Sample alone can't be resold or you can't make Sample Packs and Sell them in a different stores. Those activities are not permitted.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What is the Full Ownership license?">

- Full Ownership License- Under this license, users will own the audio that is generated. Users can reproduce, perform and re-distribute the sample. You can buy it for a single track through **License** on the track's page, and it is included with the Enterprise plan.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can you help me understand when do I need to buy a license?">
It depends on your plan.

For Free Users:

- Free users can use the content for non-commercial use.
- To use a track commercially, buy a Commercial or Full ownership license through **License** on the track's page.

For Creator subscribers:

- Every track you generate is Royalty Free.
- For sample use or full ownership of a track, buy a license through **License** on the track's page.

For Pro and Max subscribers:

- You have Royalty Free + Sample Usage to use Soundverse generated samples in your own content. No extra license is needed. Max also includes a Distribution license.
- For full ownership of a track, buy the Full ownership license through **License** on the track's page.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What counts as the human accompaniment?">
 On the Pro and Max plans, you're allowed to distribute music to DSPs with human accompaniment. 
 
 Human accompaniment could mean, adding vocals to the top, adding recording of a real instrument, changing the arrangement, editing samples etc.

 It doesn't necessary mean human vocals with full blown singing throughout the track. Even chopping of sample, or layering another sample, adding a section, adding an acappella, adding an audio effect (such as reverb, echo, distortion etc.), one shot sample, pitch change, recording another instrument on top- all of these count as human accompaniment. So yes, with a bit of "human" change - you can distribute it.

Our recommendation would be to add as much human creativity as possible to protect it. While we allow the distribution of Soundverse generated content with human accompaniment, rules for DSPs may defer company to company. 

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What do the different music rights mean?">
Music rights are generally split into several categories:
Synchronization rights: The right to add the track to a video or moving image.
Mechanical rights: The right to make copies of the audiovisual production including the track.
Public performance rights: The right to make the audiovisual production, including the track, available to the public. 
</Accordion>
